## Source-grounded construct

**SOURCE:** ABET student outcomes include identifying, formulating, and solving complex problems; engineering design under public-health, safety, welfare, global, cultural, social, environmental, and economic factors; communication; ethics and professional responsibility; teams; experimentation and data interpretation; and acquiring / applying new knowledge. ABET also requires assessment, evaluation, and continuous improvement. NCEES adds a discipline-specific professional licensure pathway.

## Accreditation-versus-competency boundary

**SOURCE:** ABET is an accreditation framework; NCEES provides licensure examinations and pathways.  
**INFERENCE:** The textbook competency is evidence-based engineering design and problem solving under constraints: frame the problem, generate and evaluate alternatives, test, interpret data, communicate, account for safety and ethics, and improve the design.  
**NOT CLAIMED:** Accreditation is licensure, or a generic engineering textbook can stand in for discipline-specific technical standards.

## Construct decomposition

1. Define requirements, stakeholders, constraints, and success conditions.
2. Frame a complex problem and decompose subproblems.
3. Generate multiple solutions and evaluate tradeoffs.
4. Use mathematics, science, tools, experimentation, and data appropriately.
5. Address safety, welfare, ethics, context, sustainability, and risk.
6. Communicate to technical and nontechnical audiences and work in teams.
7. Verify, validate, document, hand off, and improve.

## Evidence and task model

**CROSSWALK:** Design brief, requirements trace, options matrix, analysis / simulation, test plan, data interpretation, safety and ethics review, team handoff, stakeholder communication, and postmortem.  
**EVALUATOR DESIGN:** Score problem framing, constraint coverage, evidence and test quality, tradeoff reasoning, safety / ethics, communication, teamwork, and learning from failure.

## Textbook spine

`Frame → Specify → Generate → Analyze → Test → Decide → Communicate → Verify / improve`

## Failure modes and constraints

- requirements omitted or conflicting;
- single design treated as inevitable;
- simulation confused with validation;
- safety / welfare treated as a footnote;
- data used without uncertainty or calibration;
- team handoff loses assumptions;
- agent-generated design or code not independently reviewed;
- discipline-specific standards omitted.
